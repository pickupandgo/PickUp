The current contract, in one place (this corrects the brief below).
  • No app or engine ever sends a fare. Registration is POST /v1/internal/bookings { externalRef, customerSubject, quoteId, paymentMethod }. Any amount field (fare, confirmedFarePaise, …) is rejected with 400. The fare lives only in the server-issued quote.
  • Server-authoritative fare/quote. The Customer App calls POST /v1/fare/quotes; the booking carries only the quoteId; the backend freezes the quote (one amount per drop). The engine builds the trip from the priced route, and its stops must equal the quote's drops.
  • Payment methods are exactly PAY_AT_PICKUP and PAY_AT_DELIVERY. There is no “pay now / prepaid before dispatch” flow for new bookings: PREPAID_UPI is refused (422 PAYMENT_METHOD_NOT_OFFERED) and survives only for historical rows. The “when does a prepaid customer pay” question is therefore closed.
  • PAY_AT_PICKUP is paid at the pickup before handover: DRIVER_ARRIVED → payment required → booking PAID → PICKUP_VERIFIED. An unpaid handover or start is refused with 409 PAYMENT_REQUIRED.
  • PAY_AT_DELIVERY is paid per drop, at the drop the driver has reached, for that drop's own amount, before that drop's handover. One drop's payment never satisfies another. The booking goes UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID (not “only after the last drop”).
  • paymentGate is backend-derived. The backend returns { stage, stopSequence, paymentState, amountPaise, handoverAllowed }; the engine and Driver App only read it, and a handover is allowed only when handoverAllowed is true. The engine never sends PAYMENT_CONFIRMED, an amount, a fee or a payment status.
  • Gated OTPs. OTPs come only from the authenticated owner route GET /rides/:id/otps and are withheld (null) until the gate allows that handover. They are gone from the unauthenticated POST /rides / GET /rides/:id, the OTP-skipping POST /trips/:id/pickup/confirm path is deleted, and OTPs are never logged.
  • Firebase on every call. customerSubject / driverId come from the verified Firebase token, never from the request body.
  • Backend-first, durable outbox, persistent engine. Gated transitions apply only after the backend's 2xx; the engine reports through a durable, FIFO-per-trip, idempotent outbox with backoff and persists its bookings (ENG-<uuid> refs) and outbox in its own PostgreSQL. Registration, assignment, every step and the outbox are idempotent: one winner per race.

In one paragraph

The payment backend is finished and tested: it holds the confirmed fare, takes the customer's money through Razorpay, records cash and QR collections at pickup or delivery, calculates the platform fee, keeps driver earnings and runs weekly payouts to verified bank accounts. It is the only component allowed to decide that money has arrived. It currently knows nothing about real bookings, because the trip engine has never told it about one. Until the engine calls the server-to-server API described in section 5, no customer can pay, no driver can collect, and no earning exists. This document is everything needed to close that gap.

2. What blocks real money today

These are findings from the current engine-v2 source, not guesses. Each one must be fixed.

#ProblemWhereConsequence with real money
1 The engine accepts a fare from the app. rides.service.ts, createRide:
const fare = dto.fare || fareEstimate.fare		 A modified or repackaged app sets its own price. Once the engine registers bookings, that is the amount actually charged. This is the single most serious issue.
2 Booking ids are sequential and held in memory. rides.service.ts: private rides: RideRequest[] = [], id R${rideCounter} Ids restart at R1 after every deploy or crash. A new booking then collides with an old, possibly already paid, booking. All trip and payment history is lost on restart.
3 No authentication anywhere; identity is taken from the request. main.ts (only app.enableCors()); customerId, driverId read from body and path Anyone can act as any customer or driver: read trips, accept rides, verify OTPs, cancel, trigger payment flows. Not publishable to either store.
4 No booking exists before a driver accepts. Customer app matching.ts creates one ride per candidate driver; the accepted ride.id becomes the booking Prepaid payment has nothing to attach to. Each rejected offer leaves an orphan ride. Cancellation and refund have no stable reference.
5 The engine never contacts the payment backend. No call to /v1/internal/* anywhere in engine-v2 The payment backend has no booking, so there is nothing to pay, no fee, no earning and no payout. This is decision D-51 in the backend's open-decisions log.
6 Two different offer timeouts. 30 s auto-reject in createRide; 15 s RIDE_EXPIRY_MS in findPendingForDriver, which only runs while a driver app polls An offer's real lifetime depends on whether a driver app happens to be polling. Dispatch behaviour is not deterministic.
Do not switch Razorpay to live keys before items 1 to 3 are fixed.

With a client-supplied fare and no authentication, a live integration can be made to charge the wrong amount, or to charge the wrong person. The backend already refuses some of this (it never trusts an amount sent by an app, and it verifies every payment with Razorpay), but the fare it stores is whatever the engine registers. Garbage in, charged out.

The two rules that hold this together

1. The fare is frozen at confirmation. The engine calculates it, registers it once in paise, and it never changes. The backend stores fare_estimate_minor equal to final_fare_minor and a database constraint enforces that. Every later amount (the Razorpay order, the QR, the cash figure the driver sees, the platform fee, the driver's earning) is derived from that one number.

2. Completion is gated on payment. The engine reports COMPLETED when the job is physically done. If the booking is not paid, the backend refuses with 409 PAYMENT_REQUIRED and the engine retries later. A trip can never finish with money outstanding, whichever payment method was chosen.

Payment methods

Backend valueCustomer App labelWho collects, and when
PREPAID_UPI Pay now (UPI, card, netbanking, wallet) The customer pays in the app through Razorpay Checkout. Covers every Checkout method, not only UPI.
PAY_AT_PICKUP Cash at pickup The driver collects cash, or shows a booking-linked UPI QR, from arrival until delivery. Handled entirely by the Driver App.
PAY_AT_DELIVERY Cash on delivery Same, but only after the last drop is delivered.

The engine only has to register which method was chosen. It does not implement collection: the Driver App and the backend already do that, including the platform fee debit for cash trips.

4. Engine changes (the core work)

4.1 mustCalculate the fare on the server and ignore the client's

Delete the client fare path. In createRide (or its replacement, see 4.3):

// before
const fare = dto.fare || fareEstimate.fare;

// after
const fare = fareEstimate.fare;   // server-calculated, authoritative

Then remove fare from the request DTO entirely, so a client sending it gets a validation error rather than being silently ignored. Same for any other money or identity field a client should not control.

The existing fare logic is fine to keep as-is. It already returns whole rupees (Math.ceil), so conversion is exact: 

confirmedFarePaise = fareRupees * 100   // integer, no rounding, no floats
Never use floating point for money.

Compute in integer paise end to end. The backend rejects anything that is not an integer, and rejects amounts below 100 paise (₹1) or above 50,000,000 paise (₹5,00,000) — Razorpay's own per-order limits.

4.2 mustAuthenticate every request with a Firebase ID token

Identity must come from a verified token, never from the request body or path. Use the same Firebase project as the Driver App and payment backend, so one identity works everywhere:

SettingValue
Firebase projectpickup-7fabe
Issuerhttps://securetoken.google.com/pickup-7fabe
Audiencepickup-7fabe
Public keys (JWKS)https://www.googleapis.com/service_accounts/v1/jwk/securetoken@system.gserviceaccount.com

Verification rules (the payment backend follows exactly these; mirroring them keeps behaviour consistent):

  • Algorithm must be RS256. Reject anything else, and reject alg: none.
  • The kid must match a currently published key. Cache the key set and refresh it on a miss.
  • Verify iss, aud, exp and iat. Allow a few seconds of clock skew, no more.
  • The user id is the sub claim (the Firebase UID). That value is what the payment backend calls customerSubject / driverSubject.
  • On any failure return 401 with no detail about why.

The easiest correct implementation is the Firebase Admin SDK (firebase-admin, verifyIdToken) behind a NestJS guard. Then customerId and driverId come from the token, and a caller can only ever act as themselves. Ownership must be checked on every read too: a customer may only fetch their own trips, a driver only theirs.

4.3 mustCreate a real booking at confirmation, before dispatch

Introduce a booking that exists before any driver is contacted, stored in a database (PostgreSQL is already in the stack for the payment backend):

FieldRule

Dispatch offers become children of the booking rather than separate rides. Rejected or expired offers do not create new bookings, so there is exactly one booking, one fare and one payment per customer request.

Move dispatch to the server.

Today the Customer App picks candidate drivers and offers the ride to each in turn. That has to move into the engine: the app should confirm a booking and then poll its status. While the client chooses the driver, a modified client can choose any driver, and offer timeouts cannot be enforced. Pick one offer timeout (section 2, item 6) and apply it server-side on a timer that does not depend on a driver app polling.

The key is a server secret.

It lives only in the engine server's environment or secret store. It must never be in an app build, in client-side code, in a repository, in a log line, or in an API response. Anyone holding it can register bookings and move trip states. If it leaks, tell Raj immediately: it is rotatable (the backend accepts a comma-separated list, so a new key can be added before the old one is withdrawn, with no downtime).

Error response shape

Every failure, on every endpoint, has this body. Branch on error.code, never on the message text.

Log requestId against your booking. It is the fastest way for Raj to find the matching server-side record when something needs investigating.

5.5 post/v1/internal/trips/{tripId}/status — lifecycle

POST /v1/internal/trips/f37a7b5b-.../status
{ "status": "DRIVER_ARRIVED" }

Statuses the engine may send, in this exact order:

DRIVER_ASSIGNED (set by /assign, not sent by the engine) | DRIVER_ARRIVED driver reached the pickup point | PICKUP_VERIFIED pickup OTP verified | IN_TRANSIT loaded and moving | DROP_PROGRESS arrived at a drop / working through stops | DELIVERED last drop delivered | PAYMENT_CONFIRMED <-- BACKEND ONLY. Never send this. | COMPLETED job finished; refused until the booking is paid
BehaviourDetail
Strict orderSkipping a step is 409 INVALID_TRIP_TRANSITION (COMPLETED excepted, see below).
Repeats are no-opsSending the current or an earlier status returns 200 and changes nothing. Retry freely.
PAYMENT_CONFIRMEDOwned by the backend. Sending it is a validation error. When a paid booking reaches DELIVERED, the backend advances to PAYMENT_CONFIRMED itself, in the same transaction.
COMPLETEDAllowed only from PAYMENT_CONFIRMED. If the booking is unpaid: 409 PAYMENT_REQUIRED, nothing changes, and the engine must retry later. On success the booking closes and the driver's earning is created atomically.
Cancelled tripsAny status on a cancelled trip is 409 INVALID_TRIP_TRANSITION.
The COMPLETED retry loop is a required feature, not an error path.

For cash and QR bookings the driver may still be collecting when the goods are delivered. The engine should attempt COMPLETED, and on 409 PAYMENT_REQUIRED retry on a schedule (for example every 15 s for a few minutes, then every few minutes, with a cap) until it succeeds or the booking is cancelled. A background reconciliation job should also sweep trips stuck at DELIVERED. Without this, delivered-and-paid trips never close and drivers never get their earning.

5.6 post/v1/internal/bookings/{bookingId}/cancel

POST /v1/internal/bookings/7c2e4a10-.../cancel
{ "reason": "No driver accepted the request" }

reason is 3 to 200 characters and is stored. Cancels the booking and its trip, and closes any open payment QR at Razorpay. 200, idempotent. A completed booking cannot be cancelled (409 BOOKING_CLOSED).

Call it for: customer cancellation, driver-side cancellation, dispatch exhausted with no acceptance, and any engine-side abort.

Cancelling a paid booking does not refund it.

The backend records the cancellation, flags the booking for refund review and alerts, but refunds are not automatic (decision D-22 / D-52). For prepaid bookings this is exactly why the prepaid timing question in section 11 matters: if dispatch runs after payment and then finds no driver, someone has a refund to process by hand.

6. End-to-end flows

6.1 Prepaid, paid before dispatch timing pending

This matches the master specification ("PREPAID_UPI / online before booking") and is the recommended default.

#ActorAction
1Customer AppBuilds the booking. Asks the engine for a fare estimate.
2Customer AppConfirms with payment method PREPAID_UPI. Sends no fare.
3EngineRecalculates the fare, writes the booking, calls POST /v1/internal/bookings, stores paymentBookingId, and returns it to the app with the booking ref.
4Customer App(Raj's side) POST /v1/bookings/{paymentBookingId}/payment with an Idempotency-Key, opens Razorpay Checkout, then POST .../payment/verify. Only the server's PAID counts.
5EnginePolls GET /v1/internal/bookings/{id} until paymentStatus = PAID. Then calls /searching and starts dispatch.
6EngineDriver accepts → /assign → store tripId.
7EngineReports each status through DELIVERED. The backend auto-advances to PAYMENT_CONFIRMED because the booking is already paid.
8EngineCOMPLETED succeeds first time. The driver's earning is created.
9BackendEarning waits for Razorpay to settle the money to Nikhil Enterprises, then becomes payable and joins the Monday payout batch.

If the customer abandons payment, cancel the booking with a clear reason and never dispatch. If dispatch then finds no driver, that is a paid booking needing a manual refund, which is the risk behind the timing decision.

6.2 Prepaid, paid after a driver accepts (alternative)

Same calls, different order: register → /searching → dispatch → /assign, then ask the customer to pay. Trade-offs:

Pay before dispatchPay after acceptance
No unpaid job ever reaches a driver. Drivers can be sent to customers who then do not pay. Needs a no-payment timeout and a cancellation rule.
"No driver found" after payment needs a refund. Refunds are rare, because payment happens once a driver is committed.
Matches the master specification. Matches how most ride apps feel to use.

Build whichever Raj picks. The API calls are identical either way, so this is a sequencing change, not a rework.

6.3 Cash or QR at pickup / delivery

#ActorAction
1EngineRegister with PAY_AT_PICKUP or PAY_AT_DELIVERY. No customer payment step.
2EngineDispatch immediately, assign, report statuses as normal.
3Driver App(already built) When the collection window opens, the driver records cash or shows a booking-linked UPI QR. Cash debits the platform fee from the driver's wallet atomically.
4EngineAttempt COMPLETED. Expect 409 PAYMENT_REQUIRED until collection finishes; retry per section 5.5.

Windows are enforced by the backend: PAY_AT_PICKUP from DRIVER_ARRIVED until DELIVERED; PAY_AT_DELIVERY only at DELIVERED. So the engine's status reporting is what opens the driver's collect button. Late or missing status updates mean a driver who cannot collect.

6.4 Cancellation and no-driver

  • Customer cancels before assignment: stop dispatch, call /cancel with the reason.
  • Customer or driver cancels after assignment: call /cancel; the backend cancels the trip too and closes any open QR.
  • Dispatch exhausted: call /cancel with reason "No driver accepted the request". Do not leave the booking open.
  • Already paid: cancellation still succeeds and is flagged for refund review. Nothing is refunded automatically.

7. Customer App: login and build

7.1 mustReal Firebase phone login

The app's login is currently a mock: LoginScreen discards the phone number, OtpVerificationScreen accepts any 4 digits, and "logged in" is a boolean in AsyncStorage. Replace it with Firebase phone authentication on project pickup-7fabe — the same project the Driver App and payment backend use.

  • Use @react-native-firebase/app and @react-native-firebase/auth (v26 line), the same as the Driver App, with their Expo config plugins.
  • In the Firebase console, add the Android package com.pickup.customer and the iOS bundle id com.pickup.customer to the existing project, then download google-services.json and GoogleService-Info.plist.
  • Add the debug and release SHA-1 and SHA-256 fingerprints for Android, otherwise phone auth fails on device.
  • OTP is 6 digits with Firebase, not 4. The existing OTP screen needs adjusting.
  • Keep the session as Firebase's own auth state (onAuthStateChanged), not a stored boolean. A stored flag can show a logged-in UI with no valid token, which then fails every API call.
  • Never store the ID token yourself. Call getIdToken() per request and let the SDK handle refresh.

Use Firebase test phone numbers during development so no real SMS is sent. The customer test account must use a different phone number from the driver test account: one phone number maps to exactly one account in the payment backend.

7.2 decisionCreating the customer account in the payment backend

The payment backend rejects unknown identities with 403 USER_NOT_PROVISIONED, and booking registration fails with 422 CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND. Today customers are created only by an ops command-line tool. Every real signup therefore needs an account created. Two options, for Raj to choose:

OptionHow it works
A. Backend self-registration (Raj's side builds it)A new endpoint the app calls once after first login with its Firebase token. The backend reads the phone from the verified token, creates the customer role only, and is idempotent. Nothing for you to build beyond calling it.
B. Engine-mediatedThe engine creates the customer in the payment backend on first booking. Needs a new internal endpoint, which is still Raj's side. No real advantage over A.

Option A is cleaner. Either way the work is on Raj's side; you need to know which call to make and when. Flagged in section 11.

7.4 Where Raj's side takes over

8. Security rules (non-negotiable)

These come from the project's master specification. They are the reason the backend is built the way it is.

RuleWhy
Razorpay secrets stay on the payment backend.Only the public key id reaches an app. The key secret signs and verifies payments; in a shipped build it can be extracted.
Never commit, log or return secrets.Includes the internal API key, Razorpay keys, webhook secrets and Firebase service accounts.
Amounts are validated server-side.The engine calculates the fare; the backend stores it and derives every other amount from it. No app ever sends an amount.
A payment-success callback is never proof of payment.The backend confirms with Razorpay (verify call, webhook or admin sync) before marking anything paid. The engine must likewise treat only the backend's paymentStatus as truth.
Ownership is checked on every request.A customer may only see and pay their own bookings; a driver only their own trips. Both are enforced in the backend's queries; the engine needs the same discipline.
Sensitive endpoints are rate-limited.Already done in the backend. Apply the same to engine endpoints that create bookings or verify OTPs.
Everything is idempotent and deduplicated.Mobile networks retry. Idempotent operations are the only safe way to avoid double charges and double payouts.
HTTPS everywhere in production.Including engine to backend.
Logs are redacted.No tokens, no signatures, no full account numbers, no OTPs.
A driver's personal UPI id is never shown.All customer money goes to Nikhil Enterprises' Razorpay account, then to drivers via weekly payouts.
Three things the engine must never do
  1. Never mark a booking paid, or infer payment from anything other than the backend's paymentStatus.
  2. Never send PAYMENT_CONFIRMED. It is the backend's own signal that money is in.
  3. Never let an app call /v1/internal/*, or hold the internal key. Those endpoints trust the caller completely.

Also worth fixing while you are in there

  • app.enableCors() currently allows every origin. Mobile apps do not need CORS at all; if a web admin panel appears later, list its origin explicitly.
  • Per-stop OTPs are stored on the trip and scrubbed from responses. Keep that scrubbing, and make sure OTPs never reach logs or the driver's payloads.
  • The Customer App's committed .env.example contains a real Google Maps API key. It should be rotated and restricted by platform and package name. Minor, but it is a live key in a public repository.

9. Errors, retries and recovery

Retry policy

ResponseRetry?Notes
Network error, timeoutYes, with backoffEvery endpoint here is idempotent. The request may already have succeeded; retrying is still correct.
503 SERVICE_UNAVAILABLEYes, with backoffDatabase busy or restarting.
409 PAYMENT_REQUIREDYes, on a scheduleExpected for cash and QR. See section 5.5.
Other 4xxNoPermanent. Log requestId, alert, and surface to ops.
422 customer/driver not found or not approvedNoAn account problem. Needs a person, not a retry.

Suggested backoff: 0.5 s, 1 s, 2 s, 4 s, capped around 30 s, with jitter. Use a persistent outbox or job queue for the calls that must eventually happen (registration, assignment, status updates, cancellation) so a backend restart or a brief outage cannot lose a booking. An in-memory retry loop dies with the process; a booking that never reaches the backend is a job nobody can pay for.

Recovery scenarios

SituationRecovery
Registration response lostGET /v1/internal/bookings/by-ref/{externalRef} to recover bookingId, or simply re-register with identical terms.
Engine restarted mid-bookingEverything needed is in your database: the ref, paymentBookingId, tripId, status. Resume from there. This is why section 4.3 requires persistence.
Backend unreachable at confirmationDo not confirm the booking to the customer, and do not dispatch. A dispatched booking the backend never heard about cannot be paid.
Trip stuck at DELIVEREDReconciliation sweep retries COMPLETED. If it stays unpaid, it is an ops matter: the admin API can inspect the full money trail for the booking.
Status update rejected as an invalid transitionRead the booking with GET, compare with your own state, and resume from the backend's position. The backend is authoritative for trip status.

10. Definition of done

Engine

  • Fare is calculated server-side only; the fare field is gone from every request DTO, and sending it is rejected.
  • Amounts are integer paise end to end. No floating-point arithmetic on money.
  • Every endpoint requires a verified Firebase ID token; customerId and driverId come from sub, never from the request.
  • Reads and writes are ownership-checked: one customer cannot see or affect another's booking.
  • Bookings, trips and offers are persisted in a database with UUID ids. Nothing important lives only in memory.
  • A booking exists at confirmation, before dispatch, with exactly one fare and one payment.
  • Dispatch runs on the server; the app no longer chooses drivers. One offer timeout, enforced server-side.
  • All six internal calls are implemented, with the API key read from the server environment.
  • paymentBookingId and tripId are stored on the booking.
  • Retries go through a persistent outbox or queue; nothing is lost across a restart.
  • COMPLETED is retried on 409 PAYMENT_REQUIRED, plus a sweep for trips stuck at DELIVERED.
  • Cancellation is reported for every path, including dispatch exhausted.
  • PAYMENT_CONFIRMED is never sent.
  • Vehicle types map to agreed fee category codes.
  • CORS is restricted; OTPs and tokens never appear in logs.

Customer App (your part)

  • Firebase phone login works on a real device, with 6-digit OTP and no mock path left.
  • Session follows Firebase auth state, not a stored boolean.
  • com.pickup.customer is registered in project pickup-7fabe for Android and iOS, with SHA fingerprints added.
  • A development build runs on device with Firebase and Razorpay native modules linked.
  • Firebase config files are present locally and excluded from version control.
  • The customer account exists in the payment backend after first login (per the section 7.2 decision).
  • The engine returns paymentBookingId to the app on confirmation, ready for the payment screens.

Joint test, before live keys

Run this on Razorpay test keys, end to end, for all three payment methods:

  1. Customer signs up with a real phone number and gets an account in the payment backend.
  2. Booking confirmed; the backend shows the booking with the expected fare in paise.
  3. Prepaid: pay in the app, verify, booking becomes PAID, trip completes, earning appears.
  4. Cash: driver records cash; platform fee is debited from the driver's wallet; trip completes.
  5. QR: driver shows the QR; after payment the trip completes.
  6. Cancellation at each stage behaves correctly, before and after payment.
  7. A tampered client (fare changed, someone else's booking id, a forged token) is refused everywhere.
  8. Kill the engine mid-booking and restart: the booking survives and resumes.
